What contributes: there is no single cause

The Public Health Agency of Canada says no single cause can explain or predict suicide. It is the result of personal, social and cultural factors that differ from person to person. Most people living with a mental illness do not attempt suicide, and not everyone who dies by suicide had a diagnosed mental illness.

Personal and health factors
  • A previous suicide attempt
  • Mental illness such as depression
  • Hopelessness or helplessness
  • Misuse of alcohol or other substances
  • Chronic pain or long-term illness
  • Trauma
Life events and environment
  • Significant loss: a relationship, a job, money
  • Unemployment or unstable housing
  • Harassment or discrimination
  • Isolation, or a lack of support
  • Not being able to reach mental health services

Protective factors work in the other direction: supportive relationships, feeling able to ask for help, and a sense of belonging. Several risk factors above, such as harassment, isolation and a lack of support, are conditions that workplaces and teams can change.

From statistics to safety: noticing earlier

  1. Make it safe to speak upClear policies, trained leaders and a culture where raising a concern carries no penalty. In Nova Scotia, employers have needed a written workplace harassment prevention policy since September 1, 2025.
  2. Notice change earlyA short daily check-in shows how mood, stress, energy and sleep change over time. A very low mood or very high stress brings up a private message with support options.
  3. Connect people to help fastCrisis numbers are one tap away, and facilitators have step-by-step guides for moments such as someone telling them something worrying.
  4. See the pattern, protect the personAnonymous weekly summaries show strain across a group, so leaders can fix workloads and conditions. They see groups of 10 or more people only, never individuals.
What to listen for
  • Talking about not being around much longer, or about being a burden
  • Feeling trapped or hopeless
  • Pulling away from people, or giving belongings away
  • More alcohol or drug use
  • Sudden calm after a period of distress

If you hear this, ask directly and calmly: “Are you thinking about suicide?” Asking does not put the idea in someone’s head. Then listen, stay with them, and connect them to help.
